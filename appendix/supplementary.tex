\section{Health-index development comparators}
The development identifiers P03--P06 refer to archived exploratory pilots: minimum departures (P03), initial tolerance (P04), empirical and terminal-reconstruction comparisons (P05), and transfer/refitting in FD003 (P06). These pilots precede the main-protocol freeze.

For sensor $j$, the causal summary is $\bar x_{ij,15}(t)=15^{-1}\sum_{u=t-14}^{t}x_{ij}(u)$. Orientations, population scales and fitted coefficients are learned outside the evaluation engines; each engine supplies its own initial sensor reference. P03 investigates sensitivity of the detection assumptions while holding prognostic predictions fixed, together with an initial HI proxy. P04 introduces a tolerance for initial sensor variation. For oriented departure $a_{ij}(t)=d_j[\bar x_{ij,15}(t)-b_{ij}]$, with $b_{ij}=\bar x_{ij,15}(20)$ and $d_j\in\{-1,1\}$ learned from fitting engines, the margin-based candidates take the form
\[
\widehat H_i^{\min}(t)=\min_j\operatorname{clip}
\left(1-\frac{[a_{ij}(t)-A_j]_+}{B_j-A_j},0,1\right),
\qquad t\geq20.
\]
Here $[u]_+=\max(0,u)$, $A_j$ is a statistical initial tolerance and $B_j>A_j$ is an empirical terminal reference learned from fitting engines. Neither is a certified operational limit. For fixed references and channels, the margin reaches zero if and only if $a_{ij}(t)\geq B_j$; increasing $A_j$ alone cannot correct terminal anchoring. The HI is unavailable before its initial reference, and P is not used to impose its shape.

The terminal-reconstruction comparator uses patterns $\mathcal V_k$ from the final observed cycles of fitting engines. Its reconstruction and residual are
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal K_h(\mathbf z,\mathbf v)
&=\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2h^2}\sum_j\frac{(z_j-v_j)^2}{\nu_j^2}\right],\\
\widehat{\mathbf z}_F(\mathbf z)
&=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal V_k}\mathcal K_h(\mathbf z,\mathbf v)\mathbf v}
{\sum_{\mathbf v\in\mathcal V_k}\mathcal K_h(\mathbf z,\mathbf v)},\qquad
D_F(\mathbf z)=\|\mathbf z-\widehat{\mathbf z}_F(\mathbf z)\|_2,\\
\widehat H_i^{\mathrm{AAKR}}(t)
&=\operatorname{clip}\left(\frac{D_F(\mathbf z_i(t))-c_F}{c_H-c_F},0,1\right),
\qquad c_H>c_F.
\end{aligned}
\]
The scales $\nu_j$ describe terminal-pattern dispersion. The initial anchor $c_H$ is the residual at the initial reference; $c_F$ is the median fitting-engine endpoint residual obtained while excluding that engine's patterns from reconstruction and metric estimation. Causal summaries and deterministic sensor-subset candidates replace the original study's feature-extraction and evolutionary-search components. Accordingly, the experiment compares an adaptation of terminal AAKR \cite{Baraldi2018HI}, not the complete published method.

\section{Empirical support and perturbation replays}

The empirical-support diagnostic uses consecutive raw sensor increments, not HI values or moving averages. For each fitting engine, let $\Delta\mathbf x_i(t)=\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_i(t-1)$ and $\mathbf m_i=15^{-1}\sum_{t=6}^{20}\Delta\mathbf x_i(t)$. Pool the residuals $\mathbf r_i(t)=\Delta\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf m_i$ over fitting engines and cycles 6--20. Their sample covariance $\mathbf V$ uses denominator $N-1$; retain channels $J=\{j:V_{jj}>10^{-12}\}$, independently of HI channel selection, and set $\nu_j=\sqrt{V_{jj}}$. The standardised fitting residuals are $\mathbf v_i(t)=(r_{ij}(t)/\nu_j)_{j\in J}$. Let $\mathbf R$ be their sample covariance and $\mathbf P$ its inverse restricted to eigenvalues greater than $10^{-12}$. With $Q_{.99}$ denoting the linearly interpolated empirical 0.99 quantile, define
\[
 L_M=Q_{.99}\{\mathbf v_i(t)^\top\mathbf P\mathbf v_i(t)\},
 \qquad L_j=Q_{.99}\{|v_{ij}(t)|\}.
\]
For an original or perturbed evaluation trajectory $\mathbf x_i^{(v)}$, the evaluated increment is $\mathbf e_i^{(v)}(t)=([\Delta x_{ij}^{(v)}(t)-\bar m_j]/\nu_j)_{j\in J}$, where $\bar{\mathbf m}$ is the mean of the fitting-engine centres $\mathbf m_i$. Thus fitting residuals are centred per engine, whereas evaluation increments use the common fitting centre. An increment is inside if and only if
\[
 I_i^{(v)}(t)=\mathbf1\!\left\{
 (\mathbf e_i^{(v)})^\top\mathbf P\mathbf e_i^{(v)}\leq L_M,
 \quad |e_{ij}^{(v)}|\leq L_j\ \text{for every }j\in J\right\}=1.
\]
The global perturbation adds a fixed channel-shift vector multiplied by $g(t)=\operatorname{clip}((t-20)/20,0,1)$. The local replay ends at the original decision $C_0$, or $T_i-1$ if no original decision exists, and uses $g(t)=\mathbf1\{t>20\}\operatorname{clip}((t-C_0+19)/19,0,1)$. Both leave the initial reference unchanged. For the global replay, each trajectory's prefix ends at its own decision, or $T_i-1$ without a decision; in the local replay the fallback is $C_0$. A paired replay is wholly inside support only when
\[
 \prod_{t=6}^{C_0} I_i^{(0)}(t)
 \prod_{t=6}^{C_v} I_i^{(v)}(t)=1,
\]
where $C_v$ is the perturbed prefix end. Destination cycle 6 and both ends are included; F is excluded. Date-shift summaries require both decisions to exist, whereas decision-presence summaries retain missing decisions. This whole-prefix condition differs from support at individual common sensitivity times. Separate 0.99 cut-offs do not imply 99\% joint or whole-prefix coverage. Exceedance denotes statistical extrapolation relative to initial increments, not physical impossibility; inclusion does not establish physical plausibility.

\subsection*{Coefficient stability metrics}

Stability of the coefficients is also measured between the folds of each repetition, from the stored models: the Spearman correlation of $\beta_j$, not $w_j$, over the channels retained in both folds, the share of channels with the same orientation, the Jaccard overlap of channels with $\beta_j>10^{-9}$ and the overlap of the three largest $\beta_j$ values. Ties in the latter are resolved by sensor number. This diagnostic differs from the contribution-rank comparison above. Stability is examined by excluding each fitting engine in turn, refitting orientation and coefficients, and recording the shift of the first-passage landmark. Four alternative coefficient formulations were compared on development engines only and then through the full chain; none is adopted.

\section{Earlier scaled renewal scenarios and restoration expenditure}

This section preserves the earlier scale-bridge analyses. They are sensitivity scenarios, not the literal-flight scenario now used in the main text. The sources concern engines of 19,500--33,000 lb thrust, whereas the benchmark engine class is 90,000 lb; the reference interval therefore does not physically calibrate the benchmark engine.

The developmental scale analysis used a partition-specific age control, whereas the final three-arm comparison uses the single 127-cycle benchmark gate. They are different references. With the central scale $\xi=40.8$ in FD001 and 34.1 in FD003, 1,600 operational flight cycles per year and an LLP counter of 20,000 cycles, the earlier age control needed 6.64/7.25 visits per position over 20 years and lower-anchor ISO 5.51/5.43: reductions of 17.0\%/25.1\%. About 1.6 LLP visits were common to all rules. Without LLP, reductions stayed at 21.3--22.3\% and 29.0--29.8\% across the scale grid. ISO gains over the landmark were small; 36\% of FD003 cells were indistinguishable. A literal one-to-one interpretation in the earlier, partition-age experiment gave approximately 150--240 restorations per position; that is not the reported central scenario or a new three-arm simulation.

An operational cycle is counted as engine start, flight, landing and shutdown. C-MAPSS records a snapshot per simulated flight; $\xi=L_{\mathrm{ref}}/\bar T$ rescales the degradation lifetime to a declared operational service-interval scenario. The benchmark gate therefore becomes $127\xi$ operational cycles, not 127 operational cycles. The public \texttt{cycle\_scale.csv} gives both quantities by subset. There is no simulated intra-flight start or shutdown process.

All three arms share the same utilisation, horizon, initial-age condition, LLP counter and restoration assumptions. Let $N^{\pi}_{20}$ count restorations after degradation removals or forced LLP removals over 20 years. At the common illustrative cost $c_R$, expected restoration expenditure is $c_R\mathbb E[N^{\pi}_{20}]$. The central $c_R$ is US\$6 million; US\$5/7 million multiply every central cost by $5/6$ or $7/6$. The public cost table uses the unrounded archived simulation means. Costs have no discount or inflation; inspections, monitoring and downtime have no incremental charge, and endpoint events have no added consequence cost. Thus this is a comparison of restoration expenditure under a fixed-cost model, not total maintenance cost or company profit.

Inspection is not assumed costless in practice. Its zero incremental cost is the experimental contract. If engineering data later justify a nonzero inspection charge or different restoration scope after different removals, the total-cost comparison must be extended. No such new scenario or optimal policy is inferred here.

The earlier four-subset simulation used 5,760 cells over scale, utilisation, LLP, initial age and restoration effectiveness, with 2,000 replicates per cell. The first run failed its predeclared stationary check ($\max|z|=6.15$) because the initial life distribution misweighted stochastic outcomes. That run was preserved, the weighting corrected and a test added; the corrected run passed ($\max|z|=3.90$, long-horizon error at most 0.24\%, identical LLP counts across arms). An independent scalar implementation reproduced 192 sampled cells exactly. Across the full grid, conservative ISO versus age retained the expected sign in 11,518/11,520 comparisons, and the hybrid versus the same PHM rule in 11,519/11,520; the remaining comparisons were indistinguishable. These are scenario contrasts, not independent replications.

\section{Literal-flight renewal scenario}

Following the author's explicit scenario choice, one C-MAPSS cycle is assumed to represent one flight per engine ($\xi=1$). A twin-engine aircraft accrues one cycle at each position per flight. At 1\,600 flights per year, each position accumulates 32\,000 cycles in 20 years; expected restoration expenditure for two equivalent positions is twice the single-position total, without implying independent engines or an aircraft-level risk estimate. The mapping is a scenario assumption, not physical calibration of the degradation rate.

P20.2 uses only the archived complete-engine decisions: 100/180/100/169 engines in FD001/FD002/FD003/FD004 per repetition. Three repetitions reuse engines. All five PHM controls, their inspection variants and the age rule are retained for execution leads one and two. The central comparison keeps always-valid monitoring, full restoration, a mature initial fleet, an independent LLP counter of 20\,000 flights and the original inspection generator (entry 127, interval five, $W=60$, $\gamma=1$, $p_P=0$, $p_F=1$). Cost is US\$6 million for every restoration, including LLP, with zero incremental inspection charge and no other costs. No models, margins or removal rules were refitted or selected.

The protocol was written before this execution and after confirmation. For each of 12 subset/repetition cells, 2\,000 Monte Carlo replicates share engine/outcome streams across all 22 arms. Initial lives are sampled with length weighting and residual age uniformly along the chosen life; LLP initial age is uniform on its counter. Each subsequent life draws an engine and then an outcome. The eight numerical core functions match the corrected earlier simulator. All 264 arm/cell combinations passed the stationary no-LLP check (maximum $|z|=2.3035$, threshold 4.5); LLP visits were identical between arms in each replicate. Independent scalar calculations checked 121\,406 outcome atoms and reproduced all five counts in 12 replicates per arm/cell (3\,168 comparisons). Deterministic age removal was checked at both new and mature starts without LLP.

Table~\ref{tab:twenty} and Table~\ref{tab:restoration-cost} give the literal-flight totals. Conservative ISO uses 149.5--191.1 restorations per position versus about 252.8 for age removal, and the same rule with inspection 159.4--197.1. The conditional mean FD004 event count falls from 0.8855 to 0.0188 per position with inspection. Point-anchor ISO has 10.4--24.8 events without inspection and 0.0018--0.6353 with it. No event outcome is removed from the restoration count. The conditional Monte Carlo standard error of any displayed mean visit count is below 0.064; it excludes population and model-selection uncertainty. The large absolute costs reflect the short assumed flight lives of the benchmark under the literal mapping and must not be interpreted as operator forecasts. The direction of the central visit comparisons agrees with the earlier scaled scenario; this does not establish physical equivalence or accepted risk.

The separate public package \texttt{literal\_cycles\_2026-10-19} contains selected decision inputs, a portable numerical core, the runner and independent verifier, results, conditional Monte Carlo errors and hashes. It is a new exploratory series following the 32-series review in S3, and is not part of that earlier 1,531-record ledger.

\subsection*{Post-landmark updates and temporal features}
Updating five cycles after the landmark reduced empirical-HI forecast RMSE from 17.85 to 17.11 cycles in FD001 and from 14.84 to 14.31 in FD003. However, comparisons at family-specific landmarks mix representation and timing: synchronising the margin and empirical HIs reduced their FD003 RMSE difference to $-1.05$ cycles, with interval $[-2.96,0.69]$. With RF in FD003, temporal sensor statistics reduced RMSE from 16.44 to 14.40 in 100 complete engines ($-2.04$; $[-3.63,-0.42]$) and from 15.69 to 11.21 in the 22 truncated engines with a landmark ($-4.48$; $[-8.23,-1.98]$). These gains were local to the evaluated states: HI-history gains did not persist at the last official observation, Ridge did not improve, and slope alone matched or slightly improved on the full HI history. All contrasts remain exploratory.

\subsection*{Selected HI thresholds and waiting times}
A threshold or a waiting time on the HI, selected from development engines alone, added 13.08--15.49 cycles (FD001) and 8.29--12.90 (FD003) to the marker for the threshold, and 8.50--10.00 and 6.90--9.00 for waiting, across repetitions and both execution leads. Threshold rules had 0--2 endpoint events per 100 engines in each subset, and waiting-time rules 0--1; four of the 240 internal selections were infeasible. These controls recover more life than immediate landmark removal, but neither their gains nor those of the point-anchor forecast establish a preferable policy under a common validated risk constraint.

\subsection*{Synthetic multi-regime normalisation test}
With one regime the normalisation reproduced the original FD001 HI and forecasts in all ten folds, with a maximum forecast difference below $10^{-7}$ cycles. In a synthetic test with six regimes injected into FD001, the raw pipeline lost the landmark in 27\% of the engines and increased the landmark RMSE by 66\% (Table~\ref{tab:regime-test}). With the normalisation, performance returned to within 1.4\% of the original. $K$-means recovered the injected regimes without error. This is a necessary and not a sufficient condition, because the regimes were well separated and the effects were additive and multiplicative per sensor. Interaction between regime and degradation is not represented.

\section{Further method and result details}
The following paragraphs are retained in full from earlier versions of the main text, which now summarises them.

All 9\,600 original action prefixes contained an increment outside initial empirical support. Consequently, no paired prefix met the whole-prefix inclusion condition among the 1\,084\,160 nonzero global replays or the same number of local replays. These are engine--partition--policy--perturbation comparisons, not independent engines. Both decision dates existed in 1\,059\,726 global and 840\,064 local comparisons; none qualified for a support-restricted date-shift estimate. This establishes statistical extrapolation relative to initial increments, not physical implausibility, and does not validate physical robustness.

Excluding single fitting engines flipped five sensor orientations in 13 of 25 cases in one repetition and moved the landmark by 204--213 cycles. Coefficient refitting alone shifted it by up to 179; optimality checks did not indicate numerical failure. Internal stabilisation reduced the median partition p95 shift from five to three cycles and the largest shift from 223 to 45 in FD003, but worsened maxima in 8/30 FD001 and 3/30 FD003 partitions. In the full-chain evaluation, selected variants gained 1.30--1.96 mean cycles in FD001 and 2.29--4.74 in FD003 for conservative ISO, with positive paired intervals. Landmark RMSE improved in 11 of 12 contrasts, partly with later prediction times. Between-repetition p95 landmark range changed from 9.15 to 12.05 cycles in FD001 and from 27.45 to 21.60 in FD003, and FD003 gained endpoint events. These mixed results failed the pre-specified transfer criterion; the working HI was retained.

A post-confirmation descriptive check of the archived ISO decisions separated finite and infinite margins without refitting. Across repetitions and both execution leads, the finite-margin rule acted at its first usable opportunity in 55--86\% of FD001 evaluations, 72--100\% in FD003 and 90.6--100\% in FD002. The corresponding incremental mean life over the marker was 0.21--1.57, 0--0.83 and 0--0.29 cycles. In FD004, first-opportunity actions accounted for 99.3--100\% of finite-margin evaluations; all 17 infinite-margin evaluations per execution lead acted immediately, and both groups had zero incremental life. The denominator includes engines without a decision. Thus, the limited gain also occurs with finite margins; infinite margins enforce landmark removal for part of FD004 but do not alone explain the finding. This analysis describes the tested entry rule, reserve and calibration rather than the value of forecasts under other policies.

When the visibility of the damage varies between engines, so that inspections of the same engine are dependent, the cost of the arm rises. With a window varying by $\sigma=0.25$, the rate increase of the lower-bound rule was 3.9\%, 3.9\%, 4.1\% and 7.5\% in FD001 to FD004 (3.1--6.7\% with independent inspections), and with $\sigma=0.5$ it was 5.3\%, 5.5\%, 5.9\% and 9.3\%; for the point-anchor rule it rose from 10.8--12.5\% to 11.4--14.1\%. A latent shape had little effect. The expected events of the point-anchor rule with inspection stayed below 0.4 per 100 engines in every variant. The qualitative conclusion is unchanged, although differences in damage visibility increase the restoration-rate penalty.

The five-cycle interval is a frequent-inspection scenario in simulated time, not an interval derived from an approved engine maintenance programme. European Union Aviation Safety Agency (EASA) airworthiness directive (AD) 2019-0249R1 requires inspections of specified Trent 1000 intermediate-pressure compressor parts within five flight cycles after defined asymmetric-power or depressurisation events \cite{EASA2019_0249R1}. This is an event-triggered compliance deadline, not a repetitive five-cycle inspection interval, and does not calibrate the inspection model used here. By contrast, EASA AD 2025-0206R1 imposes repetitive borescope inspections of specified Trent 1000 high-pressure turbine blades at no more than 50 flight cycles, reduced to ten after defined crack findings \cite{EASA2025AD0206R1}. The five-flight cadence therefore lies below the documented repetitive range and represents a favourable inspection-capability scenario rather than a representative interval; its admissibility rests on the declared damage window, since five cycles plus the reserve and execution lead remain below the smallest window tested.

The one-cycle-per-flight assumption used for the illustrative 20-year totals does not physically calibrate the inspection cadence. The interval remains fixed at five benchmark cycles in the frozen comparison, interpreted as five flights in the literal scenario. Its influence has not been assessed by interval sensitivity analysis, which, with representative inspection costs and downtime, would be needed before operational interpretation. The reported events, utilised life, inspection counts and restoration demand are conditional on the assumed cadence and rejection model and do not establish an operational inspection schedule.

\section{Details moved from the main text for length}
The paragraphs and tables below are retained in full from the version of the main text that preceded the length reduction for submission.

Each HI family uses an increasing isotonic converter \cite{Barlow1972} fitted on separate engines, $\widehat r_i(t)=g_{c,k}(\widehat H_i^c(t))$, with $c\in\{\min,\mathrm{emp},\mathrm{AAKR}\}$. Development selection minimises RUL error at $50\%$, $70\%$ and $90\%$ of each complete trajectory. The three retrospective locations span horizons from an earlier prognostic opportunity to near the recorded endpoint, without tying the choice to one instant. Sampling is confined to complete development trajectories: the fractions are not physical degradation boundaries, and operational prediction and removal do not use the eventual lifetime. The development folds use 40 fitting, 20 converter, 20 selection and 20 evaluation engines. Final official-test comparisons use 60/20/20 training engines, with choices fixed before test scoring. Test labels score only the last observed cycle, without reconstructing future sensors.

FD003 has one operating condition and two degradation modes, involving the high-pressure compressor and fan. Direct transfer preserves FD001 parameters and converters; refitting learns them from FD003 training engines. Lifetime distributions also differ, so this exploratory contrast does not isolate the effect of the second mechanism.

$\alpha=0.5$ was set at the exploratory midpoint of the bounded empirical scale, between $\mathrm{HI}=1$ and $\mathrm{HI}=0$, so that forecasting could start from a reproducible intermediate condition. P, which is capability-dependent and is not identified in C-MAPSS, need not be estimated for that choice. The value is the numerical midpoint of the index, not the temporal midpoint of the P--F interval, half the remaining useful life, a reliability of $50\%$, or a physically validated degradation threshold. Even the conventional training target satisfies $H_i^*=0.5$ at $\RUL=\rho\,2^{-1/\eta}$, which depends on the chosen scale. Adequacy is read from landmark availability, remaining lead to the recorded endpoint, error in predicted F, later prediction updates, sensitivity to the HI formulation and the removal outcomes that follow.

The sum is rectified before the HI is clipped; individual contributions remain signed. For a decomposition of the bounded loss, the radial allocation is $a_{ij}=c_{ij}\operatorname{clip}(S_i,0,1)/S_i$ when $S_i>0$ and zero otherwise, giving $\sum_j a_{ij}=1-\widehat H_i$. Explanations are assessed for their reproduction of the HI, converter and action chain.

Coefficient stability uses foldwise ranks, orientation agreement, active-channel overlap and the three largest coefficients; leave-one-engine-out refits assess landmark shifts. Supplementary Material S2 gives the exact metrics. Alternatives were selected in development before full-chain evaluation.

The empirical-support diagnostic compares consecutive raw sensor increments with the initial fitting-engine increment distribution, using simultaneous Mahalanobis \cite{Mahalanobis1936} and channelwise limits. A paired perturbation is classified as wholly inside only if every increment in both original and perturbed decision prefixes passes both limits. Supplementary Material S2 gives the centring, covariance, cut-offs and replay definitions. These are empirical extrapolation diagnostics; inclusion does not establish physical plausibility, and separate 0.99 cut-offs do not imply 99\% whole-prefix coverage.

A 20-year horizon is used only to illustrate repeated exposure. With one cycle per flight ($\xi=1$, not calibrated), 1\,600 flights per year and an independent LLP counter of 20\,000 flights \cite{AC33701}, each position accumulates 32\,000 cycles; the simulation, its validation and the earlier scaled scenarios, which use $\xi=L_{\mathrm{ref}}/\bar T$ with a reference interval of 8\,425 flight cycles from maintenance-cost analyses \cite{AC2003,AC2005,AC2019}, are in Supplementary Material S4 and S5.

For illustration only, at one cycle per flight and 1\,600 flights per year, removal at 127 flights implies about 253 restorations per position in 20 years against 149--191 for the conservative ISO rule, and 159--197 with inspection added (Supplementary Material S5). These totals follow arithmetically from the rates, depend on the cycle mapping and are implausibly high for real engines; only the comparisons between arms are interpreted.

In the earlier uncertainty experiment, adding slope improved RF over current HI in each FD003 repetition, with descriptive paired intervals excluding zero, but did not consistently outperform ISO. For nominal 90\% bilateral bands, calibrating the maximum error along the trajectory increased FD003 RF-slope whole-path coverage from 60--82\% to 86--90\%, at the cost of wider bands, with no evidence on removal risk.

A complementary diagnostic of the stored models includes all four subsets; the FD002 and FD004 models come from the confirmation reported in the main text. Between folds of the same repetition, the median Spearman correlation of $\beta_j$ was 0.28--0.35 (FD001), 0.51--0.67 (FD002), 0.47--0.51 (FD003) and 0.39--0.50 (FD004). The orientation was the same in all retained channels for FD001 and FD002, in 94\% for FD004 and in 100\% for FD003 except in the first repetition, where it fell to 67\%. The overlap of channels with $\beta_j>10^{-9}$ was 0.79--0.86, 0.63--0.73, 0.44--0.50 and 0.40--0.46, and the three largest $\beta_j$ values agreed in a third to two thirds. The coefficients are therefore only moderately stable, the less so in the subsets with two fault modes, and an explanation by individual sensor coefficients should be read with that variability. Repetitions reuse the same engines, so these figures are not independent.

The instability of the landmark was only partly concentrated in long-lived engines, and not enough to justify excluding any engine. The Spearman correlation between life and the between-repetition range of the landmark was 0.171 (FD001; interval including zero) and 0.283 (FD003); relative range did not grow with life, and endpoint events, counted descriptively, were more frequent in short lives. No engine was excluded. A single age limit of $A_{\mathrm L}=127$ cycles was set. None of the 200 examined engines has $T_i\leq127$, so the upper confidence limits for $\Pr(T\leq A_{\mathrm L})$ are 0.80\%, 1.14\% and 1.49\% at confidence 80\%, 90\% and 95\%. For FD001 alone the 95\% limit is 2.95\%. These limits are in-sample.

The landmark of an engine varied by a median of 6 (FD002) and 9 (FD004) cycles across repetitions, with maxima of 31 and 40, without a range comparable with that of engine 55 of FD003.

Fixed reserve-12 and nominal-95\% sensitivities increased utilised life in development without observed endpoint events (the main text). In FD002 they added only small gains; in FD004 conservative ISO lives and events were unchanged. These sensitivities were not selected as replacements for the main rules; Supplementary Material S3 and the public contrast tables retain them.

With the extension over the monitoring-loss grid, the lower-bound ISO rule keeps a saving against the 127-cycle limit of 24.5--25.3\% (FD002) and 40.9--41.2\% (FD004) with always-valid monitoring, 21.4--22.1\% and 33.8--34.0\% at $u=0.005$, and 15.1--15.4\% and 21.3--21.5\% at $u=0.02$. The pattern reproduces the development subsets, and the larger saving in FD004 reflects its longer lives.

Three candidate tasks are evaluated: removal at 127 cycles; sensor-only removal from the landmark, forecast anchor, reserve and execution lead, with the monitoring-loss response in the main text; and the hybrid addition of five-flight inspections from flight 127 under the literal mapping, where rejection can only advance removal. Each specifies entry, trigger, execution lead and conduct on monitoring loss.

Within the assessment of alternative tasks framed by IP-180, the present evidence covers the benchmark mechanisms and their declared scenarios. IP-180 limits AHM use to tasks that are not safety-related and do not cover certification maintenance requirements, and excludes AHM-supported evolution of Maintenance Review Board Report (MRBR) task intervals \cite{IP180}; Issue Paper 197 (IP-197) assigns certification to on-aircraft features and validation of ground processing to the operator's authority \cite{IP197}. Because the C-MAPSS endpoint represents exhaustion of operability margins, the failure-effect category of the removal task, and hence its eligibility under IP-180, remains undetermined. Physical failure limits, representative inspection capability, operational monitoring validity and engineering evidence for task applicability also remain to be established. The comparison therefore provides candidate task formulations and identifies the evidence needed for further assessment; it does not complete an IP-180 applicability or approval process.
